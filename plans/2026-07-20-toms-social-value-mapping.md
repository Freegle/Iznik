# Using the National TOMs framework for a Freegle social impact statement

*Research + mapping, 2026-07-20. Follows up Natalie's TOMs enquiry. Freegle figures are the canonical calendar-2025 set from the impact-report pipeline (dashboard API, derived 2026-06). TOMs facts verified against primary PDFs (council mirrors of the framework, Social Value Portal's NT2022 update guidance, the National Social Value Taskforce's own VCSE guide) — sources listed at the end.*

---

## 1. Is TOMs free? Yes — with one nuance

- The **framework itself is free**. The National Social Value Taskforce's own guide states: *"The National TOMs is a free resource"*, and the framework PDF is mirrored without any paywall on many council sites (Richmond, Southampton, Stroud, Lancashire, the LGA itself). It was developed in 2017 by the Taskforce, sponsored by the LGA, with Social Value Portal (SVP) as steward.
- The widely-repeated claim that it's under a **Creative Commons licence could not be verified at primary source** — no reachable official page states an actual CC variant. What is solidly confirmed is "free to download and use". (The Taskforce site nationalsocialvaluetaskforce.org was unreachable; that's where a formal licence statement would live.)
- **What costs money**: SVP's commercial "TOM System" platform — bid management, dashboards, and crucially **independent third-party verification of reported figures** ("included as standard with every membership"). Free users self-attest; paying members get their numbers checked.
- **Free middle tier**: SVP's **"Open Access TOM System"** is explicitly free for **Public Sector and VCSE organisations** — Freegle qualifies. It's registration-gated (app.socialvalueportal.com) and includes the measure workbook. **This is the recommended route to get the current proxy £ values**, which are otherwise only published in dated PDFs.
- **Recommendation: pay nothing.** Use the free framework + register for the Open Access tier to get current proxy values. Paid membership only becomes relevant if a funder ever demands SVP-verified figures — and a council partner that's already an SVP member can often run the validation from their side.

### Framework drift to be aware of (it has moved on since the 2021 PDF)

| Edition | What changed |
|---|---|
| NT2017–2021 | 5 themes, ~20 outcomes, 38–48 core measures (the version most council PDFs mirror) |
| **NT2022** (1 Jun 2022) | Restructured into **Light (18) / Core (40) / Non-core (~280)** measures; all proxy values re-based; new Environment measures incl. **NT88 (waste reduced through reuse)** — see below |
| 2024 update | Guidance streamlined; "social dimensions" added to NT1 (local jobs) and NT18/19 (local spend); Open Access tier expanded |
| Oct 2025 | "**Global TOM System**" launched (international extension, not a UK reissue). SVP progressively rebranding "National TOMs" → "**Social Value TOM System™**" |

Also distinct: the UK Government **Social Value Model (PPN 06/20 → PPN 002, new model from 1 Oct 2025)** is a separate, government-owned framework for **central** government procurement. TOMs is the local-authority-world framework. The NHS has its own Social Value Model, but NHS bodies (e.g. East of England Ambulance Service) map it onto bespoke TOMs — so TOMs fluency travels.

### Is there precedent for a charity (not a bidder) using TOMs?

Yes — explicitly endorsed by the framework's own stewards. The Taskforce's *"Getting the most from Social Value: a step-by-step guide for SMEs and VCSEs"* tells VCSEs to measure what they already do against TOMs **independent of any bid**, and even suggests VCSEs substitute a certified SROI multiplier for the default £1 proxy on £-invested measures. The Open Access tier being open to VCSEs reinforces this.

## 3. What Freegle can report — the mapping

Canonical calendar-2025 figures, TOMs-coded. Carbon uses the NT2022 proxy (£244.63/tCO2e), which is reassuringly close to the DESNZ £260 figure the impact report already uses.

| TOMs ref | Measure | Freegle 2025 quantity | TOMs value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NT88** | Waste reduced through reuse (£ equiv value) | 10,910 tonnes ≈ **£11.1m** equivalent value (WRAP £1,019/t) | ~£11.1m |
| **NT31** | CO2e savings | 5,564 tCO2e × £244.63 | **~£1.36m** |
| **NT29** | Volunteering hours for community | ~6,900 moderator hours (412k actions × 1 min — conservative) × £14.80 | **~£102k** |
| **NT28** | Donations/in-kind to community projects | Brighton Free Shop (38t, 52k visitors), bulk clearances | case-by-case £ |
| **NT27** | Vulnerable people helped to live independently | Cost-of-living programmes (e.g. Wandsworth: £15k grant → £78k community value) | programme £ invested |
| **NT16/17** | Equipment / hours donated to VCSEs | items channelled to foodbanks, furniture-reuse charities via partnerships | needs counting |
| NT50–53 | Innovation | the platform itself as reuse infrastructure | narrative / co-designed |

**Cumulative since 2016** (only-rises framing): ~89,000 t reused ≈ £90m NT88 equivalent; ~45,000 tCO2e ≈ £11m at the NT31 proxy.

**What Freegle cannot meaningfully claim**: the Jobs theme (NT1–13 count direct FTE hires — Freegle's paid team is tiny) and local-spend measures (NT18/19 — expenditure is only ~£133k). The jobs story (WRAP/Green Alliance: reuse supports 8–20 jobs per 1,000 t vs 0.1 for landfill) stays as narrative evidence, not a TOMs line — TOMs has no "jobs supported in the wider economy" measure.

**Future measures worth building data for**: NT32 (car miles saved — would need a survey/assumption on collection vs delivery of new goods) and NT87 (plastics) — neither currently measured.

### Integrity rules when TOMs-coding (consistent with the impact-report rules)

1. **One tonnage everywhere** — NT88 and NT31 both derive from the same 10,910 t. TOMs treats waste-reuse and carbon as distinct outcomes and does allow both lines, but disclose they arise from the same tonnes.
2. **Don't stack frameworks** — a TOMs-coded total is an *alternative expression* of the same impact already published as WRAP-based value. Never add TOMs £ to WRAP £.
3. **Attribution** — TOMs' no-double-counting principle means each unit of delivery can be claimed by only one organisation. If a council claims Freegle-delivered tonnes in *its* social value reporting, Freegle shouldn't simultaneously present those tonnes as its own claim in the same assessment exercise (fine in Freegle's own annual statement).
4. **Additionality** — TOMs values what goes beyond business-as-usual. For Freegle's own statement this is easy (none of this happens without the platform); for partner claims, the partner's marginal contribution is what counts.
5. **Verification honesty** — state that figures are self-reported against the framework, from platform-measured weights, with WRAP conversion factors; independent verification is available via SVP membership if ever required.

## 4. How to use it — two routes

**Route 1 — Freegle's own TOMs-coded impact statement.** A one-page annex to the annual impact report: the table in §3, coded NTxx, with a per-council version generated from the dashboard's per-council tonnes. Because ~a third of English/Welsh councils already assess social value in TOMs vocabulary, this lets a council officer drop Freegle's numbers straight into their own reporting. This is cheap: the data pipeline already exists; it's a re-presentation layer.

**Route 2 — TOMs as a partnership sales tool (probably the bigger prize).** Councils and corporates have social value *commitments* they must deliver against TOMs measures. Freegle can offer itself as a delivery mechanism:
- A council that funds a Freegle partnership (à la Wandsworth) can report the resulting tonnes/CO2e/volunteering under its own TOMs targets.
- A contractor with a TOMs commitment in a council contract could deliver NT16 (equipment donated to VCSEs) or NT28 (in-kind community donations) by routing surplus office/site goods through Freegle bulk clearances — and Freegle can hand them the evidenced £ figure they need.
- Pitch line: "Partnering with Freegle generates auditable social value against NT88, NT31, NT28, NT29 — here's last year's per-tonne yield."

**Concrete next steps**
1. Register (free, VCSE) for the Open Access TOM System to obtain the current proxy-value workbook — resolves the stale-proxy problem before anything is published.
2. Add a "TOMs annex" page to the impact-report template (the §3 table + integrity notes), national + per-council.
3. Give Natalie a short "social value offer" sheet for the Councils toolkit: which NTxx measures a partner can claim by working with Freegle, with worked examples (Wandsworth, Brighton Free Shop).
4. Only revisit paid SVP membership if a specific funder demands independently verified figures.

**Critical gap: NT31 (carbon) and NT88 (waste reuse) are NOT in the free tier** — Freegle's two headline environmental measures sit in the Core set, which this account cannot select. Other account limits: no account CSV export, cannot download the full TOM System guide (per-set guidance PDF is available), not eligible for tender bidding.

**What this means for the strategy:**
1. **Community measures — use the portal.** NT29 volunteering at the live £17.48/h proxy values Freegle's ~6,868 moderator hours at **~£120k** (up from ~£102k on the stale £14.80). NT28 (in-kind £, incl. equipment) and NT117 (environmental £ invested) are usable per-programme.
2. **Environment headline — three options.** (a) Quote the published NT2022 framework proxies (£244.63/tCO2e) from the free PDFs, disclosing vintage — the framework itself is free to cite. (b) Ask SVP for Core-set access — the account page explicitly invites VCSEs to "get in touch" for more measure sets; worth one email before concluding it costs money. (c) Keep the environment story in Freegle's existing WRAP/DESNZ framing and use TOMs coding only for the community/volunteering layer.
3. **The partner route is unaffected and is the strongest play.** Councils/contractors with paid SVP memberships have the full measure set including NT31/NT88 in *their* accounts — Freegle supplies the evidenced quantities (tonnes, CO2e, hours, per council area) and the partner claims them against their own commitments. Freegle never needs the paid tier for that.

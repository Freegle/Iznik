## Freegle's social value in 2025, in National TOMs terms

Freegle is a UK reuse charity (Community Benefit Society 32410R, HMRC-charitable XT32865): a free platform where people give unwanted items to others nearby instead of throwing them away. Around 500 local communities, moderated by volunteers, at a total running cost of about £133,000 a year.

The National TOMs framework ("Themes, Outcomes and Measures", National Social Value Taskforce/LGA) is the social value vocabulary used by roughly a third of councils in England and Wales. Expressed against its measures, Freegle's 2025 delivery was:

| TOMs measure | What we delivered in 2025 | Social value |
|---|---|---|
| **NT88** — waste reduced through reuse of products and materials† | **10,910 tonnes** of goods passed between neighbours instead of being disposed of | **~£11.1m** equivalent value (WRAP-based £1,019/tonne) |
| **NT31** — carbon (CO2e) savings† | **5,564 tonnes CO2e** avoided (WRAP factor: 0.51 tCO2e per tonne reused) | **~£1.36m** (at £244.63/tCO2e, NT2022 proxy) |
| **NT29** — volunteering hours for local community projects | **~6,900 hours** of volunteer moderation keeping 500 communities safe and active (a deliberate under-count: 412,000 logged actions at one minute each) | **~£120,000** (at £17.48/hour, live 2026 portal proxy) |
| **NT28** — donations and in-kind contributions to community projects (£1 per £, incl. equipment) | Brighton Free Shop: 52,000 visitors, 38 tonnes; bulk clearances routing surplus goods to local groups | reported per project |
| **NT117** — support for environmental & biodiversity conservation (£1 per £ invested incl. time) | environmental programmes, e.g. National Reuse Day delivery | reported per programme |
| **NT27** — vulnerable people supported to live independently† | Cost-of-living partnerships, e.g. Wandsworth: a £15,000 grant generated £78,086 of community value (5.2×) | reported per programme |
| **NT15** — expert support to VCSEs (£106.34/hr, live portal proxy) | advice hours to community groups via the Councils & Partnerships programme | needs counting |

† Not in the free Open Access tier (Light set of 12): these lines use the published NT2022 framework proxies and say so. NT29/NT28/NT117/NT15 use the live 2026 proxies from Freegle's own portal account.

Since tracking began in 2016, Freegle has recorded a cumulative **~89,000 tonnes** reused (~£90m equivalent value) and **~45,000 tonnes CO2e** avoided.

**How to read these figures.** The waste (NT88) and carbon (NT31) lines both arise from the same measured tonnage — TOMs treats them as distinct outcomes (resources vs climate) but we disclose the overlap and never add them into a single headline. All figures are self-reported from Freegle's platform data (weights recorded only on confirmed exchanges, a known under-estimate) using published WRAP conversion factors; independent verification is available through Social Value Portal membership should a partner require it. These TOMs figures are an alternative expression of the same impact published in Freegle's annual impact report — use one framing or the other, never both added together.

## What a partner can claim by working with Freegle

Councils, housing providers and contractors carry binding social value commitments scored in TOMs measures. Partnering with Freegle is a low-cost, evidenced way to deliver against them:

- **Fund a Freegle community partnership** (as Wandsworth did): the resulting tonnes reused (NT88), carbon saved (NT31) and volunteering generated (NT29) in your area are measured continuously by our platform, per council area, and we hand you the evidenced figures for your own reporting.
- **Route surplus goods through Freegle** (office moves, refits, void clearances): claim the £-equivalent value as equipment donated to VCSEs (**NT16**) or in-kind community donations (**NT28**), with per-item evidence.
- **A worked yield figure**: every 1,000 tonnes reused through Freegle ≈ £1.02m NT88 equivalent value + 510 tCO2e ≈ £125k NT31 carbon value, delivered at a fraction of typical social-value programme cost.

One attribution rule, honestly applied: each unit of delivery is claimed once. Where a partner claims Freegle-delivered outcomes in their reporting, we attribute those units to the partner rather than double-counting them in our own claims within the same assessment.

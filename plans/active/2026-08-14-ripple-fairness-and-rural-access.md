# Rippling fairness and rural access: implementation spec

## 1. The decision

Keep the way posts get mailed out and crossposted into other groups exactly as it works today, with the same nearest-4,000-people cap. On top of that, unchanged, add a new fallback check that runs only when a member is BROWSING for posts (looking at the map or the feed) or trying to reply to one they found. If a member fails the normal check, and they are either (a) someone from a sparse or medium-density area whose own settings already let them travel further than the current post's audience limit reached, or (b) someone from a more deprived area who is meant to get extra reach under a fairness rule, we let them see and reply to that post anyway. Nobody extra gets emailed and no group gets an extra copy of the post because of this change. It only affects what a member can find if they go looking. This targets the two real, separately measured problems: rural members like Mod-John being shut out of posts they are easily close enough to reach, and posts from deprived areas being under-represented in what a deprived member ends up shown, without touching how many people get pushed a notification.

## 2. Composition rule: fairness versus rural access

The two mechanisms never fight over the same thing because they are gated onto two mutually exclusive situations for a given post, decided by a single existing fact the routing server already computes: did the 4,000-person headcount cap actually bind for this post, or not.

**Rule, precisely:**

For every post, look at `total_freeglers` (the true count of people within the 45-minute ceiling, already computed and already returned uncapped today) versus `target_users` (4,000). Exactly one of two things is true:

- **Cap bound** (`total_freeglers > target_users`, i.e. `effectiveTotal < total` in the routing code): this post computes and stores a **rural-access lane** only. No fairness lane is computed for this post, ever, regardless of `RIPPLE_FAIRNESS_ENABLED`.
- **Cap did not bind** (`total_freeglers <= target_users`): this post computes and stores a **fairness lane** only, and only if `RIPPLE_FAIRNESS_ENABLED` is on. No rural lane is computed, because the rural lane would be a no-op here anyway (the audience already reaches the full 45-minute ceiling, so there is no gap between what was capped and what the person's own band entitles them to).

A single post therefore never has both lanes. Two engineers implementing this independently would both write: "check `total_freeglers > target_users` once, at schedule-build time, and branch on that boolean to decide which of the two lane-builders to run, if either."

**Why this is the right split, not an arbitrary one:** this is where MEASURE (this session's own audience-mix analysis) put the two problems. Capped/governed reaches are already close to quintile parity (Q1 16.8-20.7% against a 20.2% population share, depending on the cut). The demographic deficit lives specifically in ceiling-bound reaches (Q1 11.9% against 20.2% expected, a 41% relative shortfall). Rural exclusion (Mod-John) is specifically a cap-bound phenomenon: his post capped at 28.0 minutes while his own budget is 45. So each lane is aimed at exactly the regime where its problem was measured to exist, not applied uniformly and hoped to help.

**What "conflict" means here, and the answer:** the only place these two ideas could conflict is at the top, over whether the audience cap itself should be raised, lowered, reweighted, or removed to serve one goal or the other. The answer is: **the cap always wins for anything pushed to a member (mail, digest, crossposting into a group).** It is never reweighted by quintile and never widened by rural band. Both new lanes are strictly additive on top of whatever the cap already produced, visible only to a member who actively looks. There is nothing to reconcile at the push layer, because neither lane ever changes what gets pushed.

**Tie-breaker if compute cost has to be trimmed:** the rural lane costs nothing extra (it is sliced for free out of the isochrone Step 1 already computes for every post). The fairness lane costs one genuinely new Dijkstra calculation per ceiling-bound post. So the only real cost lever is the fairness weight `W`. If the routing server's CPU budget is under pressure, reduce `W` first (see rollout), not the rural lane, both because that is where the actual cost is and because, independent of cost, rural under-reach is self-diagnosable (a mod noticing their own feed is thin, exactly how Mod-John's case surfaced) while a deprived member's under-reach is not (it just looks like "fewer posts"). If a genuine forced choice ever arises where cutting the free lane would help, protect the fairness lane, not the rural one, for the same reason.

## 3. The audience cap: kept, unchanged

`target_users=4000` stays exactly as it is. Nothing in this design reweights, stratifies, or removes it.

**The honest argument for keeping it:**

- MEASURE's own data says the cap is not where the demographic problem lives. Capped reaches are already near-proportionate (Q1 20.7% against a 20.2% population share in the governed sub-sample). Reweighting the cap by quintile would be solving a problem that measurably is not there, while risking the exact "tighter cap in denser areas" shape that a prior design pass explicitly tested against real collection outcomes and refuted (a tight people-count cap caught only 46% of real collectors versus 81% for a looser one).
- Removing the cap reopens the original, already-solved problem verbatim: the ~80x population disparity for equal drive-time that produced the Discourse #9808 complaints the cap was built to fix (1,089 notifications from 80 mostly-London posts before the cap existed).
- There is no replacement cost control if it is removed. `reply_saturation_stop` (default 5) triggers on distinct repliers, not on notification volume, so it does nothing to stop the first mail wave. Reach-mail generation was measured at roughly 75% of `ripple:expand`'s per-tick wall-clock even under today's 4,000 cap. The sandwich-bounds polygon architecture exists specifically because an untruncated version of the browse query measured 30-50 seconds and returned no posts to clients, and an old full-polygon London schedule response hit ~24MB. An uncapped 45-minute isochrone in a dense city would be larger than anything that architecture has been tuned against.
- Both problems Edward actually named, rural access and fairness, are reachable through the browse-only overflow lanes at a fraction of that cost and none of that risk. Disabling the cap is therefore not necessary, and this design deliberately makes it unnecessary rather than avoiding it by fiat.

If a future measurement shows the overflow lanes are not enough, the next lever to pull is band-conditioning `target_users` by the post's own origin density (raising it for medium/sparse origins only, leaving dense at 4,000), not removing it outright. That is a real, separate design decision this spec does not make, flagged in section 9.

## 4. The Mod-John case, worked through under this design

Post 120838351, Frankley, B45 0BJ, origin 52.407/-2.0127, origin IMD quintile 1. Mod-John, user 43899574, Franche, Kidderminster, 52.40057/-2.256841, IMD quintile 4, sparse band (`browseMaxMinutes: 45`, `browseReachMaxDistance` untouched, i.e. the unlimited sentinel), 31.4 minutes by road from the post.

**Committed audience (mail, crosspost): unchanged.** The stored polygon still caps at 28.0 minutes, `cumulative_users` still exactly 4,000. Mod-John is not mailed and his group is not crossposted this post, exactly as today. This design does not touch that.

**Which lane does this post get?** `total_freeglers` for this origin at 45 minutes is 8,259, well above `target_users=4000`, so the cap bound. This post computes a **rural lane** only, three rings (dense/medium/sparse ceiling, 20/30/45 minutes) sliced for free from the Step 1 isochrone that already ran to 45 minutes for every post since PR #1308.

**Does Mod-John clear it?** His band is sparse, so he is checked against the sparse ring, the real routed 45-minute isochrone from Frankley. His actual distance is 31.4 minutes, comfortably inside 45, and the pool at 45 minutes (8,259 members) is known to include him (at 31.4 minutes the cumulative count is roughly 4,955, well past his position). He passes: he can now find and reply to this post by browsing, and a reply attempt is no longer held.

**Does the fairness lane matter for him?** No, and it should not: this post never computes one (cap-bound posts only get the rural lane), and even if it did, his own quintile (4) only earns a modest stretch (1.25x at W=1) that his 45-minute band already covers on its own. His exclusion was a headcount problem, not a deprivation one, and the design routes him to the mechanism that actually matches that.

**What this changes for him in numbers:** nothing about the 4,000 people mailed. One new outcome: browsing the map or feed, or attempting to reply, now succeeds instead of silently failing (or being held) for a member whose own settings already say 45 minutes is fine.

## 5. File-by-file change list, ordered for independent testability

**Step 0 (independent, ships first, no dependency on anything else): persist per-member deprivation quintile.**
- `iznik-routing-go/server.go`: add `handleQuintileLookup(g *Graph)`, registered as `v1.Get("/quintile", ...)` next to the existing `/fairness` and `/drive-time` routes (near line 478). Returns `{"quintile": int}` from `g.Deprivation.Lookup(lat,lng)` (`deprivation.go:61`). No Dijkstra, no new data load.
- Test: `curl` the endpoint directly against a known LSOA centroid, confirm it returns the expected quintile. This is fully testable in isolation before anything else changes.
- `iznik-batch/app/Console/Commands/Browse/BackfillBrowseMaxDistanceCommand.php`: add a memoized `quintileFor()` call (same `RADIUS_MEMO_DP`-style rounding pattern as the existing radius lookups), writing a new `settings.deprivationQuintile` key for every member. Also capture the pre-sentinel numeric band radius the command already computes and currently discards under the `DISTANCE_UNLIMITED` override, persisting it as `settings.bandCeilingMinutes` (this is just `browseMaxMinutes` made explicit and reliable even for maxed-out sliders; a small, separately useful fix regardless of the rest of this design).
- Test: run the command with `--dry-run` against a sample, confirm both new settings keys populate correctly and match expected band/quintile for known test locations.

**Step 1: routing server, rural lane (no extra Dijkstra, cap-bound posts only).**
- `iznik-routing-go/ripple.go`: in `handleRippleSchedule`, after Step 4's existing truncation (unchanged), check `total > effectiveTotal` (the cap bound). If so and `rural_access=1` was passed, slice three polygons out of the already-computed `iso.ReachedNodes` at 20/30/45-minute thresholds, reusing the existing polygon-building code the tick schedule already uses (not `bounds.go`'s `IsochroneBounds`, which is unrelated and not part of this pipeline today, and not a "reachbounds.go" file, which does not exist in this repo). Return these three polygons in the JSON response as `overflow_rural: {dense: wkt, medium: wkt, sparse: wkt}`.
- New query param `rural_access` (default off), parsed next to `target_users`.
- Test: call the endpoint directly with `rural_access=1` against a known dense origin, confirm the three polygons appear and are supersets of each other (dense ⊂ medium ⊂ sparse) and that with the flag off the response is byte-identical to today. This is a pure routing-server change, fully testable with `curl` against the local routing server, no batch or apiv2 involvement needed.

**Step 2: routing server, fairness lane (one extra Dijkstra, ceiling-bound posts only).**
- `iznik-routing-go/ripple.go`: when `total <= effectiveTotal` (cap did not bind) and `fairness_weight=W>0` was passed, run one extra `Isochrone()` call to `maxSecs * quintileMultiplier(1, W)` (the Q1/most-generous stretch, reusing `fairness.go`'s existing, already-unit-tested `quintileMultiplier`). From that single reached-node set, slice four rings at `45min * quintileMultiplier(q, W)` for q=1..4 (skip Q5, whose multiplier is always 1.0, i.e. no stretch, already covered by the primary polygon). Return as `overflow_fairness: {q1: wkt, q2: wkt, q3: wkt, q4: wkt}`.
- `iznik-routing-go/fairness.go`: factor the existing W-clamp (currently inlined in `handleFairness`) into a small shared `clampFairnessWeight()` used by both the existing endpoint and this new code, so there is exactly one place the [0,1] clamp lives.
- Test: call with `fairness_weight=0` and confirm it is a byte-identical no-op (this is the dark-ship correctness proof: `quintileMultiplier(q,0)=1` for every q). Call with `fairness_weight=1` against a known ceiling-bound origin and confirm the four rings nest correctly (q1 largest, q4 smallest, all larger than the flat 45-minute primary polygon). Independently testable against the local routing server.

**Step 3: Laravel config and ReachService wiring.**
- `iznik-batch/config/freegle.php`: add `'rural_access' => ['enabled' => (bool) env('RIPPLE_RURAL_ACCESS_ENABLED', false)]` and `'fairness' => ['enabled' => (bool) env('RIPPLE_FAIRNESS_ENABLED', false), 'weight' => (float) env('RIPPLE_FAIRNESS_WEIGHT', 0.0)]`, siblings of the existing `extent` block.
- `iznik-batch/app/Services/Ripple/ReachService.php`: constructor reads both flags once (mirroring the existing `targetUsers` read at lines 59-61). `scheduleParams()` appends `rural_access=1` and/or `fairness_weight=W` to the outgoing request only when enabled, mirroring the existing `target_users>0` conditional-inclusion pattern exactly.
- Test: unit test on `scheduleParams()` output with each flag combination, confirming the outgoing HTTP params match expectations, with no live routing server or database needed.

**Step 4: storage.**
- New migration: nullable `rippling_reach.overflow_bounds` (JSON) and `rippling_reach.fairness_weight_applied` (float). A single JSON column rather than 7 separate geometry columns, because these are consulted only in the fallback branch (never on the hot, indexed path the primary polygon/outer_bound/inner_bound serve), so they do not need spatial indexing, matching the existing convention for `rejected_groups`/`reachable_group_ids` (also plain JSON columns on this table).
- `iznik-batch/app/Services/Ripple/ExpandService.php`: write both new columns in **all three** SQL write paths from day one, not just the init INSERT: the init INSERT (~1078-1106), the `advanceDue` UPDATE (~1266-1271), and the `recomputeReach` shrink UPDATE (~305-309). This directly avoids repeating `density_band`'s measured ~89% permanent-NULL gap, which came from being added to only the first of these three.
- Extend the `reuse_reach` staleness guard (~958-968) with one more comparison: stored `fairness_weight_applied` against the live configured weight, alongside the existing `storedCap` check. (Rural rings need no new staleness check: they are re-derived deterministically from `max_minutes_cap`, already covered by the existing check.)
- Test: run `ripple:expand` against a test post through init, an advance tick, and a shrink, and inspect the row after each, confirming the JSON column is populated (with the correct one of `rural`/`fairness` present, never both) and never silently dropped on an UPDATE.

**Step 5: Go API read side (browse).**
- `iznik-server-go/isochrone/message.go`: `reachCandidateQuery` (~195-238). The existing WHERE is a single hand-built AND-chain string. Do **not** naively append an OR onto it (this was the concrete SQL-correctness hazard both the routing and admission ground-truth reviews flagged in the earlier proposals: `reachWhere`/`authorReachCapWhere` are pre-formed `AND ...` fragments, and literal concatenation would misassociate under operator precedence and could leak past the `ms.successful=0`/`rr.status != 'held'` filters). Instead, restructure explicitly as:
  `WHERE ms.successful=0 AND rr.status != 'held' AND ( (primary_containment AND author_reach_cap) OR (overflow_eligible AND overflow_containment) )`
  where `overflow_eligible`/`overflow_containment` resolve once per request (bound parameters, same pattern already used for `BrowseDistanceUnlimited`) from the viewer's own band (for rural) or persisted quintile (for fairness), gated by the two env flags.
- Test: unit test the SQL string construction directly (confirm the parenthesisation), then an integration test with a fixture member who fails primary containment but sits inside a stored `overflow_bounds` ring, confirming they now appear in the feed.

**Step 6: Laravel read side (reply-eligibility).**
- `iznik-batch/app/Services/Ripple/ReachQueryService.php`: add `isWithinReachOrOverflow(int $msgid, float $lat, float $lng, User $viewer): bool`. Calls the existing `isWithinReach()` first (zero added cost on the majority case that already passes); only on failure, and only when the relevant flag is enabled, checks the viewer's own band/quintile against the stored `overflow_bounds` JSON for that post. This sequential wrap (check the cheap existing path first, fall back only on failure) is the clean, low-risk pattern to use here, not a second OR-branch bolted into a shared query.
- `iznik-batch/app/Services/Ripple/RippleReplyService.php`: replace the two `isWithinReach()` calls (`shouldHold()`, the reply-hold gate, and the held-release sweep) with `isWithinReachOrOverflow()`.
- Test: unit tests on `isWithinReachOrOverflow()` directly with fixture posts/members in each state (inside primary, inside rural ring only, inside fairness ring only, inside neither), independent of any HTTP layer.

**Step 7: documentation.**
- `docs/developers/reference/rippling-algorithm.md`: document both lanes, both flags, the mutual-exclusivity rule, and the explicit scope limit (browse and reply-eligibility only, never mail, digest, or group crossposting), per the project's keep-docs-current rule.

## 6. Measurement plan

**Rural access, before/after metric:** for a matched sample of cap-bound posts (identifiable directly: `total_freeglers > target_users` on the stored row), count browse-feed impressions and reply attempts from members whose own band is sparse or medium and whose distance to the origin falls between the capped boundary and their own band ceiling. Before: this count should be at or near zero (today, no code path admits them). After: compare admitted-via-rural-lane counts against the theoretical maximum (the difference between `total_freeglers` and `target_users`, i.e. how many extra people exist in that gap). Computed by: instrumenting the new OR-branch in `reachCandidateQuery` and `isWithinReachOrOverflow` with a log-only counter (admits-via-rural per day, split by density band), which needs no new database schema, just log lines, for the first two weeks.

**Fairness, before/after metric:** re-run the exact ST_Contains-based quintile-mix methodology already validated this session (`scratchpad/rippling-fairness/analyze.js`, cross-checked against msgid 121296245's stored `cumulative_users`), restricted specifically to the ceiling-bound subgroup (the one MEASURE showed the deficit lives in), before and after enabling the fairness lane. Track: Q1 share of the audience that can actually FIND the post (via `overflow_bounds.fairness` containment, not just the stored polygon), against the 20.2% population baseline and the 11.9% pre-fairness baseline already measured this session. Also track the 30-day posts-reached-per-member figure by quintile (baseline Q1=457.6 versus Q2-Q5 average ~573.7), watching specifically whether the Q1 figure moves toward that average without Q5 gaining disproportionately.

**Sample size honesty:** the existing 60-post MEASURE sample gives a 95% CI on the Q1 equity ratio of [0.67, 1.00], suggestive but not conclusive. The phased rollout below produces much larger live samples over its first few weeks than a one-off batch query could, so proceed with the design now and let live measurement tighten the estimate, rather than waiting for a bigger offline sample first.

**Hard assertion, both lanes:** mail-queue counts, `messages_groups` insert counts, and digest recipient counts must be byte-identical before and after, in both the sampled rollout bucket and control. Any movement there indicates a bug, since neither lane is wired into mail, digest, or crossposting at all.

## 7. Rollout

**Dark ship:** both `RIPPLE_RURAL_ACCESS_ENABLED` and `RIPPLE_FAIRNESS_ENABLED` default to false. With both off, the routing server never parses the new query params, `ReachService` never sends them, `ExpandService` never writes the new JSON column (it stays NULL), and both read-side OR-branches never evaluate. Verify with a smoke test: recompute a sample of live posts' schedules before and after deploy, diff, expect zero differences.

**Kill switch:** either flag back to false, or `RIPPLE_FAIRNESS_WEIGHT=0`, instantly and fully reverts read-side behaviour, independent of whatever `overflow_bounds` data already sits on existing rows (the columns are inert without the read-side flag). No redeploy needed for a flag flip on the Laravel side; the one-time Go build/restart is needed only for the initial ship of the new endpoint and query params, not for later toggling.

**Order:**
1. **Step 0 first, alone, in production, indefinitely low-risk:** run the extended `BackfillBrowseMaxDistanceCommand` to populate `settings.deprivationQuintile` and `settings.bandCeilingMinutes`. Additive, harmless if never read.
2. **Rural lane alone,** `RIPPLE_RURAL_ACCESS_ENABLED=true`, `RIPPLE_FAIRNESS_ENABLED=false`, for 2-4 weeks. Lowest risk (zero extra routing compute, well-tested code path underneath). Watch: browse-feed query p95 latency (the sandwich-bounds architecture exists precisely because this query burned the system once), admits-via-rural log counter by density band, reply-hold release rate.
3. **Fairness lane, only once step 2 is confirmed safe,** `RIPPLE_FAIRNESS_ENABLED=true`, starting at `RIPPLE_FAIRNESS_WEIGHT=0.0` (a deliberate no-op to prove the plumbing end-to-end, since `multiplier(q,0)=1` for every quintile), then step to 0.25, then 0.5. Never ship straight to 1.0: `quintileMultiplier`'s curve has no cited empirical derivation anywhere in the codebase, and W=1's widest ring (90 minutes for Q1) is the least-tested corner of the range. Watch: routing-server CPU load specifically (this is the one genuinely new Dijkstra cost in the whole design), the Q1-vs-population equity ratio on the ceiling-bound subgroup, and the hard mail/digest/crosspost assertion above at every step.

## 8. What we are deliberately NOT doing, and why

- **Not touching mail, digest, or group crossposting.** Both lanes are browse and reply-eligibility only. A member who relies on digest mail (the large majority; only about 2.9% of active members have ever touched their distance settings, a rough proxy for who actively browses) gets no benefit from either lane unless they go looking. Extending either lane to mail was rejected because mail volume is the expensive, risky side of any reach change (measured ~75% of the per-tick wall-clock even under today's cap), and because it would reopen questions about the still-open GDPR/LIA sign-off for out-of-home-group notification that mail-widening designs would need to resolve first. A browse-only widening sidesteps that.
- **Not addressing post-origin skew.** Post origins skew Q5 30.5% versus Q1 13.2% (a 2.31x ratio), by far the largest measured skew in the whole system, larger than anything downstream of it. Nothing in this design, or in any of the three proposals it was built from, touches where posts come from. That is a giving/participation problem, not a reach-algorithm problem, and stretching the origin's own time budget (an option all three proposals considered and rejected) would grow the isochrone for every viewer of a post, not just deprived or rural ones, reopening the storage/query cost problem the sandwich-bounds architecture exists to solve, for a benefit nobody asked for.
- **Not reweighting the 4,000-person cap by quintile.** Rejected because MEASURE shows capped reaches are already close to quintile parity; reweighting there would displace a currently-included candidate to fix a problem that measurably is not happening at that mechanism, and risks recreating a cap shape a prior design pass already tested against real collection outcomes and refuted.
- **Not stratifying the cap by origin density band** (raising `target_users` for medium/sparse origins). This was a serious candidate (Proposal 2's Lever B) but the lens-2 judge showed it is keyed to the wrong axis for "rural members reaching town posts": it leaves dense origins, i.e. actual towns, at the flat 4,000 cap, so it does nothing for the literal case in the lens name. Left as a named future option in section 9, not built now.
- **Not fixing the separate `reachable_group_ids`/"mygroups" divergence.** The admission report found group targeting is capped by a third, independent mechanism from member-level admission, and that the "mygroups" browse view ignores reach polygons entirely. Real bugs, but a separate piece of work sharing only a root cause (multiple member-location sources), not touched here.
- **Not claiming this fixes the collection gap.** A separate, already-measured finding (2026-07-30 memory) shows deprived repliers are about 5.4 points less likely to actually get an item they reply to, an effect that widens with item weight, a transport/logistics signature, not an exposure one. This design improves visibility. It should not be presented as closing that gap.

## 9. Open decisions for Edward

**1. What fairness weight W to run at, once the rural lane is proven safe.** This is a values judgement, not a technical one; here are the measured consequences of each option, not a silent pick:
- **W=0** (ship dark): zero behavioural change, proves the plumbing works, costs nothing. Recommended as the mandatory first step regardless of what comes after.
- **W=0.25**: Q1 gets a 1.25x stretch (45min sparse ceiling to 56 min), Q4 (Mod-John's band) gets 1.0625x. Small, safe, likely to show only a marginal equity movement given the effect size problem below.
- **W=0.5**: Q1 gets 1.5x (up to 67.5 min), Q4 gets 1.125x. My recommendation for the first real (non-zero) step: large enough to plausibly show a measurable equity effect, still well inside the tested range of `quintileMultiplier`'s own unit tests, still leaves headroom before the untested 90-minute corner.
- **W=1.0**: Q1 gets 2.0x (up to 90 min). Not recommended as a first non-zero step: the widest, least-tested ring, and the formula's curve itself has no cited empirical derivation anywhere in the codebase, unlike every other constant in the ripple pipeline (which trace to specific measurements). If the measurement plan in section 6 shows W=0.5 is not moving the equity ratio enough, that is the point to revisit this, not before.

**2. Should the rural lane's band-matching be exact-match only (my default) or "round down to nearest ring" for the roughly 2.9% of members who have manually chosen a non-default `browseMaxDistance`?** My default, described in the design, rounds a self-chosen budget down to the nearest of the three precomputed rings (20/30/45 minutes), which never over-admits someone beyond what they asked for but can under-admit by up to a few minutes at the margin. The alternative, computing a bespoke ring at their exact chosen minute value, is more precise but re-adds real per-post compute cost for a small population. I recommend the rounding-down default unless this population turns out to matter more than expected once measured.

**3. Should the cap eventually be band-stratified by origin (Proposal 2's Lever B), in addition to these two lanes?** Rejected as the primary fix in this design (see section 8), but if the rural lane's measured uptake shows the "gap between capped and own-band" is large and persistent for sparse/medium origins specifically, raising `target_users` for those origins (dense left at 4,000, where the original disparity problem was measured) is the next lever, not cap removal. Recommendation: do not build this now; revisit only if the rural lane's own measurement shows it is insufficient.

**4. Should a rescued member ever be proactively mailed, not just able to find the post by browsing?** The honest limiter of this whole design is that most members are on digest, not active browsers. Extending either lane to `UnifiedDigestService::getPostsForUser` or `mailNewlyReachedForPost` would close that gap but reopens the mail-volume and GDPR/LIA questions this design deliberately avoided. Recommendation: do not do this in v1; treat it as a distinct, harder decision to revisit only after browse-lane data shows real, meaningful uptake worth the added risk.

**5. Is the post-origin skew (2.31x Q5-over-Q1, the largest measured skew in the whole system) worth a separate effort?** Out of scope for this spec (a ripple-algorithm change cannot manufacture posts that do not exist), but worth flagging: if genuine equity of giving is the actual goal, a product or comms intervention encouraging posting from under-represented areas is likely to matter more than anything reach-side. Recommendation: treat as a separate workstream, not blocked on or bundled with this one.

**6. The 2026-07-02 Discourse thread where "already implemented" was said about IMD-based fairness, when it was not.** Once the rural lane ships (even at W=0, since it is independent of the fairness flag), it is worth a short follow-up on that thread saying what is now actually true. A comms question for Edward, not an engineering one, noted here only so it is not forgotten.

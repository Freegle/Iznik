# Commercial customer-support platforms: multi-source intake, AI clarification, triage, ticket structuring (2026)

## Intercom Fin — procedures/workflows, pricing, nonprofit discounts

### Takeaway
Fin replaced rigid decision-tree bots with "Procedures": natural-language, agentic instructions that read/ask-for attributes, call data connectors/MCP tools, and branch dynamically; Intercom bills $0.99 per billable "outcome" (resolution, procedure handoff, or disqualification; $9.99 for a lead qualification) with no published volume discount, and nonprofit pricing exists but is undocumented and negotiated case-by-case (US-only, historically).

### Cited Findings
- Procedures are "AI-driven automations where you write natural language instructions that Fin follows... best for complex, multi-step processes that need AI reasoning," contrasted with rule-based chatbots that "follow predefined paths" — Fin is "agentic: [it] adapts when a customer interrupts, changes their request, or introduces new information mid-conversation." — [Intercom/Fin Help: Fin Procedures explained](https://fin.ai/help/en/articles/13976187-fin-procedures-explained)
- A procedure triggers when "Fin analyzes what the customer is asking and determines if it matches the 'When to use this procedure' criteria you've defined" — it cannot be triggered by a timer, button, or external event — [Fin Help: Building Fin Procedures](https://fin.ai/help/en/articles/13976254-building-fin-procedures)
- Information-gathering ("slot filling") is written as plain-language instructions, e.g. "@read email. If it is not known, ask the user for their email before continuing," plus validation instructions like flagging a too-long number as possibly a tracking number and re-asking for the order number — [Fin Help: How to write instructions in Fin Procedures](https://fin.ai/help/en/articles/13976256-how-to-write-instructions-in-fin-procedures)
- Adding a Data Connector to a Procedure makes Fin "automatically create[] temporary attributes from the connector's response," referenceable in later instructions/conditions; the older Workflows/Fin Tasks system uses an explicit "Collect data" step instead — [Fin Help: How attributes can power your Fin Tasks, escalations, workflows, and data connectors](https://fin.ai/help/en/articles/13975853-how-attributes-can-power-your-fin-tasks-escalations-workflows-and-data-connectors)
- Clarifying questions occur in three distinct places: (1) to the *admin* while drafting a procedure via "Let AI draft your procedure" (Fin grounds the draft on past conversations/docs/connectors and asks the builder clarifying questions, optional); (2) to the *customer*, disambiguating between multiple matching procedures; (3) a general low-confidence fallback where "Fin gives a disambiguation response that shares what it found, expresses uncertainty, and asks a clarifying question, or hands off to a human if it still can't resolve things." These built-in clarifying questions "cannot be edited" — builders steer behaviour indirectly via "Fin Guidance" instructions instead — [Fin Help: Building Fin Procedures](https://fin.ai/help/en/articles/13976254-building-fin-procedures); [Fin Help: Fin Procedures explained](https://fin.ai/help/en/articles/13976187-fin-procedures-explained)
- Pricing: $0.99 per billable outcome (resolution, procedure handoff, or disqualification), $9.99 per lead qualification, billed once per conversation even across multiple actions; a 50-outcome/month minimum applies only when Fin runs on another company's help desk ($49/month covers it); no volume discount, no rollover of unused outcomes — [Gleap: Intercom Fin AI Pricing Explained 2026](https://www.gleap.io/blog/intercom-fin-ai-pricing-2026)
- Seats: Essential $29, Advanced $85, Expert $132 per seat/month (annual); example: 10 seats on Advanced + 2,000 Fin resolutions/month ≈ $2,830/month total — [Gleap: Intercom Fin AI Pricing Explained 2026](https://www.gleap.io/blog/intercom-fin-ai-pricing-2026)
- A resolution can be "confirmed" (customer explicitly says it helped) or "assumed" (customer simply leaves without asking more); billing is per-conversation not per-message — [Gleap: Intercom Fin AI Pricing Explained 2026](https://www.gleap.io/blog/intercom-fin-ai-pricing-2026)
- Salesforce agreed to acquire Intercom/Fin for ~$3.6B on 15 June 2026 and completed the deal on 10 September 2026, folding Fin into Agentforce; as of 24 September 2026 the $0.99/outcome pricing was unchanged, but packaging may shift post-acquisition — [Gleap: Intercom Fin AI Pricing Explained 2026](https://www.gleap.io/blog/intercom-fin-ai-pricing-2026)
- Official resolution rate: Intercom's March 2026 blog states its average resolution rate "has increased every month and now stands at 76%"; Fin's site claims "averaging 76% across 12,000+ customers, with many seeing over 85%," "2 million weekly resolutions," "40m+ resolved conversations." Elsewhere on Fin's own site the guarantee page says the average grew "from 30% to 76% since launch," while a Sierra-comparison page states "71% across 7,000+ customers" (both live August 2026) — internally inconsistent — [Intercom Blog: From resolutions to outcomes](https://www.intercom.com/blog/from-resolutions-to-outcomes-evolving-how-fin-delivers-value/); [Update to Fin performance metrics](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/15599377-update-to-fin-performance-metrics)
- Intercom changed its resolution-rate methodology in 2026 to stop counting "Fin Constrained" conversations (ones limited by escalation rules) in the denominator, which mechanically raises the reported rate; restated historical data only goes back to 1 Oct 2025 — [Update to Fin performance metrics](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/15599377-update-to-fin-performance-metrics)
- Independent tests found much lower real-world rates: a Builts.ai test across 4 small-business clients (500 tickets/month combined, 60 days) averaged 38%, not the marketed figure; a competitor (CloneDesk) claims real-world production rates of 45–53%, lower for complex B2B queues — [Builts.ai / CloneDesk figures via getmacha.com aggregation](https://www.getmacha.com/blog/intercom-fin-ai-explained)
- Nonprofit discount: Intercom's Early Stage program page says "US-based nonprofit organizations may be eligible for a nonprofit discount," but no percentage or terms are published; a 2023 community reply from Intercom staff said the discount was only verifiable/available for US-based nonprofits; a US 501(c)(3) in 2025 was told to contact sales via the website Messenger — [Intercom Help: Early Stage program](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/9361928-early-stage-program)
- Separate published discount: the "Early Stage" startup program gives 93% off Intercom plus a year of Fin (300 outcomes + 15 sales qualifications/month included) for companies with ≤$10M raised, <15 employees, no existing Intercom account; extra outcomes, qualifications, phone/SMS/WhatsApp are billed at list price and not covered — [Intercom: Early Stage program](https://www.intercom.com/early-stage) (via third-party summary)

### Inferences
- Fin's architecture is the most explicit public example of "slot filling by natural-language instruction plus tool calls" rather than a rigid form — this is closest to what Freegle would need to replicate cheaply (structured attribute collection driven by an LLM prompt + validation instructions), without needing Intercom's own product.
- The gap between vendor-claimed (71–76%) and independently observed (38–53%) resolution rates suggests Freegle should treat any vendor's headline resolution-rate claim as a ceiling contingent on knowledge-base completeness, not a number transferable to a small volunteer-run charity's ticket mix.
- No available/public nonprofit discount schedule for Fin means Freegle cannot budget for it without a direct sales conversation; per-resolution pricing with no free tier makes Fin economically unattractive for a low-budget charity regardless of any discount, given per-seat costs are additional.

### Gaps
- No confirmed nonprofit discount percentage or eligibility terms for non-US charities (UK/Freegle) were found; only anecdotal/community reports about the US.
- No official Intercom statement on how Fin's per-outcome pricing will change after full Agentforce integration.

## Zendesk AI agents / Intelligent Triage

### Takeaway
Zendesk's Intelligent Triage classifies intent ("topic" since June 2026 rename), sentiment (5-point scale, refreshed dynamically) and language (~150 languages) from a ticket's subject + first public comment, each with a High/Medium/Low confidence score so admins can automate high-confidence tickets and route the rest to humans; AI-agent conversational resolution is billed per "automated resolution" (industry estimates ~$1.50 committed / $2.00 pay-as-you-go) on top of Suite/Copilot add-on fees, and problem/incident linking plus true duplicate detection remain largely manual.

### Cited Findings
- Intelligent triage "uses AI to automatically analyze new support tickets by classifying customer intent, sentiment, and language, and enriches tickets with actionable details, such as product names" — classification is based only on "a ticket's subject and the text of the first public comment" — [Zendesk Help: Automatically classifying customer intent, sentiment, and language](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4550640560538-Automatically-classifying-customer-intent-sentiment-and-language)
- Sentiment is scored on a five-point scale (Very positive to Very negative); with dynamic detection enabled the score updates as the customer replies; every prediction carries a High/Medium/Low confidence level so teams can "automate on high-confidence tickets and send the rest to human review" — [Zendesk Help: intent/sentiment/language](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4550640560538-Automatically-classifying-customer-intent-sentiment-and-language)
- Language detection covers ~150 languages and is on by default; Zendesk renamed "intent" to "topic" in the product in June 2026 — [Zendesk Help: intent/sentiment/language](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4550640560538-Automatically-classifying-customer-intent-sentiment-and-language)
- Intent/sentiment/language are exposed via the Ticket Fields API with confidence scores, but the AI-generated ticket Summary field is *not* accessible via API — [Zendesk Help: Can I use the API to access intent, sentiment, and language from tickets?](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/9853061174298-Can-I-use-the-API-to-access-intent-sentiment-and-language-from-tickets)
- Problem/Incident hierarchy: mark one ticket as "Problem," set matching tickets' type to "Incident" and link them; solving the Problem ticket auto-solves all linked Incidents and copies the resolving comment to them; "customers are not notified using the messaging channel," and "AI agent tickets can't be linked to problem and incident tickets." No AI-driven auto-linking was found — agents link manually — [Zendesk Help: Working with problem and incident tickets](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408835103898-Working-with-problem-and-incident-tickets)
- Duplicate detection: the Copilot add-on's "merge suggestions" identify likely duplicates from the *same requester only* (not content similarity across different requesters), surfacing up to 3 candidates at a time, requiring manual confirmation and the paid add-on; hands-off/cross-requester merging requires third-party apps (Knots, Swifteq) — [eesel AI: How to merge tickets in Zendesk](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/zendesk-merge-tickets-duplicate-conversations)
- Pricing (third-party aggregated, not all confirmed on Zendesk's own page): Suite Team $55/agent/mo, Professional $115/agent/mo (annual); Enterprise + Copilot custom; Copilot/"Advanced AI" add-on ≈ $50/agent/month layering intelligent triage, AI replies, macro suggestions on top of Suite; AI-agent conversational resolutions billed separately, estimated ~$1.50/resolution committed-plan, ~$2.00 pay-as-you-go, with a per-agent baseline allowance and automatic overage billing since January 2026 and no monthly spend cap (only lever is to pause AI entirely) — [eesel AI: Zendesk intelligent triage 2026 guide](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/zendesk-intelligent-triage); [Coworker AI: Zendesk AI Pricing 2026](https://coworker.ai/blog/zendesk-ai-pricing)
- May 2026 Zendesk repricing split resolutions into three tiers: "Assisted Escalation" (AI gathered info/routed but a human resolved — free) and "Contained Resolutions" (also free) vs. only "Verified Resolutions" consuming the paid allowance — [eesel AI: Zendesk AI agents setup, costs, rollout](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/a-complete-guide-to-zendesk-ai-agents-setup-costs-and-best-practices)
- Example total cost: a 20-agent team resolving 3,000 conversations/month pays roughly $80,000/year all-in, excluding QA ($25/agent/mo) and WFM ($25/agent/mo) add-ons — [eesel AI: complete guide to Zendesk AI agents](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/a-complete-guide-to-zendesk-ai-agents-setup-costs-and-best-practices)
- No standard nonprofit discount program exists; instead Zendesk runs "Tech for Good," a competitive grant/award scheme (partners with 110+ nonprofits globally, free software + $5,000–$50,000 grants via "Impact Awards"), gated to specific focus areas (community, crisis resilience, tech career pathways), requires proof of nonprofit status, excludes current/past Zendesk customers and grantees; 2026 applications closed 15 July 2026, results after 30 Aug 2026 — [Zendesk Tech for Good: What are the Impact Awards?](https://techforgood.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/6476814314637-What-are-the-Tech-for-Good-Impact-Awards); [Zendesk Tech for Good: eligibility](https://techforgood.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/5277614169229-What-are-the-eligibility-requirements)
- Reviewer setup complaints: G2/Capterra reviewers "repeatedly describe configuring Copilot, AI Agents, and Intelligent Triage as 'burdensome' and needing technical know-how" — [eesel AI: Zendesk AI agents 2026 guide](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/a-complete-guide-to-zendesk-ai-agents-setup-costs-and-best-practices)

### Inferences
- Zendesk's confidence-scored classification (High/Medium/Low on intent/sentiment/language) is a directly reusable design pattern for Freegle even without buying Zendesk: tag each inbound message with a confidence-scored intent/sentiment, auto-route only high-confidence cases, and route everything else to human review.
- The fact that even Zendesk — a market leader — still relies on manual problem/incident linking and same-requester-only duplicate suggestions confirms that "dedup unrelated reports of the same underlying issue into one problem" is an unsolved, mostly-manual problem industry-wide, not something to expect off-the-shelf.
- Total cost of ownership (seats + Copilot add-on + per-resolution overage + QA/WFM add-ons) makes Zendesk AI comparably expensive to Intercom Fin at scale, undermining any hope it's a "cheaper Fin alternative" for a charity.

### Gaps
- No official Zendesk-published resolution-rate statistic was found (unlike Intercom's 76% claim) to compare accuracy/marketing claims.
- Zendesk's own pricing page wasn't fetched directly; figures above are third-party estimates that "disagree" with each other, per the search results.

## Freshdesk Freddy AI

### Takeaway
Freddy AI splits into a per-seat "Copilot" for agents ($29/agent/month annual, Pro/Enterprise plans only) and a separately metered "AI Agent" charged per customer-facing session ($49 per 100 sessions after 500 free/month, once per account); nonprofit discounts exist through Freshworks sales but reported terms conflict wildly (10–20% vs 90%) and it's unclear whether AI add-ons are covered.

### Cited Findings
- Freddy AI Copilot is a "flexi" add-on at $29/agent/month (annual) or $35/agent/month (monthly), purchasable for a subset of agents rather than the whole team, but locked to Pro and Enterprise plans only (unavailable on Growth) — [eesel AI: Freshdesk Freddy Copilot pricing 2026](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/freshdesk-freddy-copilot-pricing-2025)
- Base Email/Ticketing plans: $19 / $55 / $89 per agent/month (annual); Freshdesk Omni (omnichannel): $29 / $79 / $119 per agent/month (annual) — [eesel AI aggregation](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/freshdesk-freddy-ai-pricing)
- Freddy AI Agent (customer-facing bot) is billed by session: "the first 500 sessions [are] included," then "$49 per 100 sessions," with the free allotment limited to once per account; Freshdesk Omni web-chat sessions reportedly run ≈ $100 per 1,000 sessions ($0.10/session) per independent analysis of the Omni add-ons page — [eesel AI: Freshdesk AI pricing 2026](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/freshdesk-ai-pricing)
- Example blended cost: 15 agents on Pro ($55×15=$825/mo) + Copilot for 10 agents ($29×10=$290/mo) = $1,115/month before any AI Agent session charges — [eesel AI: Freshdesk Freddy Copilot pricing 2026](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/freshdesk-freddy-copilot-pricing-2025)
- Nonprofit discount reports conflict sharply: one source claims "90% discounts on their paid plans for qualifying nonprofit organizations and educational institutions" via an application process; coupon sites instead claim 10–20% off select plans; a 2024 Freshworks Community poster who applied said the process "led to a sales call" and "sales came back with a fairly minimal discount for a high subscription compared to many other offerings" — [Freshworks Community: NPO/Non Profit Pricing](https://community.freshworks.com/getting-started-11331/npo-non-profit-pricing-39008)
- Most Freshdesk discounts "cannot be combined" — customers get "the single largest discount you qualify for," and it's unconfirmed whether any nonprofit discount extends to Freddy add-ons (Copilot, AI Agent sessions) or only the base plan — [CostBench, July 2026, via search aggregation]

### Inferences
- Freshdesk's session-based AI Agent pricing ($0.49/session equivalent once past the free 500) undercuts Intercom Fin's $0.99/resolution headline rate, making it worth a direct sales conversation for Freegle if session volume is modest and mostly simple/deflectable queries.
- Given the wide disagreement on nonprofit discount size (10% to 90%) and one real user report of only a "minimal discount," Freegle should not assume a meaningful nonprofit price break without a direct quote.

### Gaps
- No official Freshworks nonprofit discount percentage was found — all figures are third-party or anecdotal.
- No detail found on Freddy's clarifying-question / slot-filling behaviour, dedup, or problem/incident hierarchy specifically (unlike Intercom/Zendesk); would need a follow-up search of Freshdesk's own AI Agent documentation.

## Help Scout AI

### Takeaway
Help Scout's "AI Answers" is a low per-resolution add-on ($0.75/resolution, one charge per conversation regardless of how many AI replies, spend-cap controllable) layered on inexpensive base plans ($25–$75/user/month annual); it publishes an actual 10% standard nonprofit discount (stackable with the annual-billing discount) plus a separate, less-defined "Help Scout for Good" program for qualifying nonprofits/B-Corps.

### Cited Findings
- "Help Scout's billing guide says it offers a 10% discount to non-profit organizations, which can be combined with the annual payment discount" — [Help Scout Support: Billing and Plans Guide](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/596-billing-and-plans-guide)
- Separately, "the pricing page says the Help Scout for Good program offers discounts for qualifying non-profits and B Corporations"; third-party reviewer CheckThat.ai claims (unconfirmed by Help Scout) it can give "up to 100% off for organizations focused on human rights, environmental sustainability, or underrepresentation in tech" — [Help Scout Pricing](https://www.helpscout.com/pricing/); third-party claim via [CheckThat.ai](https://checkthat.ai/brands/help-scout/pricing)
- AI Answers is "available in all Help Scout plans, with a free 3-month trial. After the trial, on any paid plan, you'll be charged for each AI resolution — when the AI is able to successfully answer a customer's question without your team's help"; third-party sources put the rate at $0.75/resolution — [Help Scout Support: AI Resolutions Pricing](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1746-ai-resolutions-pricing)
- Only one resolution is billed per conversation even with multiple AI replies; a monthly spending cap can be set, and AI Answers auto-disables for the rest of the month once the cap is hit — [Help Scout Support: AI Resolutions Pricing](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1746-ai-resolutions-pricing)
- AI Drafts (agent-assist, not customer-facing) costs $50 per 100 conversations where a draft is generated, billed in 100-conversation increments; included in contact-based billing plans and current Plus/Pro plans — [Help Scout Support: AI Drafts Pricing & Billing](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1539-ai-drafts-pricing-billing)
- Base plan annual pricing (one source): Standard $25, Plus $45, Pro $75 per user/month (annual); $30/$54/$90 monthly; a Free plan exists with 5 users, 1 inbox, 1 Docs site — [featurebase.app / costbench aggregation]

### Inferences
- Help Scout is the cheapest of the major named platforms on a like-for-like basis (lowest seat price band, lowest disclosed per-resolution AI rate at $0.75 vs. Intercom's $0.99, Gorgias's $0.90–1.50, Front's "from $0.05" unconfirmed), and it is the only vendor researched with a *published, non-negotiated* baseline nonprofit discount (10%) rather than a purely discretionary/competitive grant. This makes it the most concretely "off-the-shelf affordable" AI-assisted option for a small charity, though it's unclear it does clarification/slot-filling or ticket structuring as richly as Fin's Procedures.
- It is unconfirmed whether the 10% nonprofit discount applies to AI Answers/Drafts usage charges or only seat fees — this is the single most important unresolved question if Freegle evaluates Help Scout.

### Gaps
- No information found on Help Scout AI's clarifying-question behaviour, structured-field extraction, dedup, or SLA/urgency handling — would need direct product-documentation research (docs.helpscout.com AI Answers / AI Beacon articles) beyond what surfaced here.
- No confirmation of whether the 10% nonprofit discount applies to per-resolution AI charges.

## Front

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings
- "Front costs $25 (Starter), $65 (Professional), or $105 (Enterprise) per seat per month with annual billing in 2026," down from four tiers previously — [Chatarmin: Front Pricing 2026, checked 24 Sep 2026](https://chatarmin.com/en/blog/front-pricing)
- AI add-ons: "Autopilot from $0.05/conversation, Copilot $20/seat/mo (included on Enterprise), Smart QA $20/seat/mo," Smart CSAT "$10 per seat"; Compose/Translate/Summarize are included free on all plans at 200 actions/teammate/day — [My AskAI: Front AI pricing explained](https://myaskai.com/blog/front-ai-pricing-explained)
- Autopilot is marketed as resolving "up to 70% of requests," but the "from $0.05" rate is explicitly caveated as not the real committed rate ("real rate: talk to sales") — [My AskAI: Front AI pricing explained](https://myaskai.com/blog/front-ai-pricing-explained)
- Buying Copilot + Smart QA + Smart CSAT on Professional ($65+$20+$20+$10=$115/seat) costs *more* than Enterprise ($105/seat, which bundles all three) — a deliberate upsell structure — [My AskAI: Front AI pricing explained](https://myaskai.com/blog/front-ai-pricing-explained)
- Front's AI (Autopilot, Copilot, Summarize, Topics, knowledge sources) is officially English-only — [My AskAI: Front AI pricing explained](https://myaskai.com/blog/front-ai-pricing-explained)
- Cross-vendor per-resolution comparison cited by one analysis: HubSpot $0.45/resolution, Freshdesk $0.49/session, Intercom Fin $0.99/outcome (reference price), Gorgias $1.50/automated interaction, Salesforce Agentforce $2.00/conversation — units are not directly comparable across vendors since they measure different things — [Drag Blog: State of AI Support Pricing 2026](https://www.dragapp.com/blog/state-of-ai-support-pricing/) (via search aggregation)

### Inferences
- Front's English-only AI restriction is a hard disqualifier if Freegle needs multilingual triage (Freegle's UK member base includes non-English speakers in some communities) — worth flagging even though not explicitly asked.
- No nonprofit program surfaced in this pass; absence of any hits (unlike every other vendor searched) suggests Front either has none or doesn't publicise it, making it a lower-priority option for a cost-constrained charity regardless.

### Gaps
- No nonprofit discount information found for Front at all.
- No detail found on Front's clarifying-question/slot-filling design, dedup, or channel-ingestion breadth (email/social/chat coverage was implied but not directly sourced).

## Gorgias

### Takeaway
Gorgias, aimed at e-commerce (deep Shopify integration), prices its helpdesk by ticket volume ($10–$900/month, unlimited seats) and its AI Agent per resolved interaction (~$0.90–$1.00 standard, $1.50 overage), with a documented double-counting/overage risk that can spike bills 2.7–3.5× during peak shopping periods (e.g. Black Friday); no nonprofit program found.

### Cited Findings
- "AI Agent is priced per resolved interaction, not per seat or per message. You only pay when the AI fully resolves a conversation on its own." Most plans ≈ $0.90/resolved interaction (starter plans from $1), covering 90–2,500+ automated interactions/month depending on tier — [Gorgias Blog: Gorgias AI Agent Pricing, Explained](https://www.gorgias.com/blog/ai-agent-pricing)
- Base helpdesk is billed by ticket volume, not seat: list prices "$10 (Starter) to $900 (Advanced), with Enterprise on request"; unlimited/very high user seat caps included — [eesel AI / Lindy aggregation, Gorgias Pricing 2026](https://www.lindy.ai/blog/gorgias-pricing)
- Per the Gorgias FAQ (as summarized by a third party): "a resolution also counts as a helpdesk ticket. But if the customer ends up talking to a human agent within 72 hours, the case is billed only as a helpdesk ticket, not additionally as a resolution" — a defined dedup/de-duplication-of-billing rule — [Lindy: Gorgias Pricing 2026](https://www.lindy.ai/blog/gorgias-pricing)
- Overage: tickets beyond plan limit cost $0.32–$0.40 each; AI interactions beyond the included allowance cost $1.50 each; a cited Black Friday example shows a Pro plan going from a $300 base to $1,038 due to overages, and independent analysis estimates 2.7–3.5× cost spikes during BFCM-style peaks — [eesel AI / Lindy aggregation]
- Reviewer-estimated automation rate: 26–56% of tickets resolved by AI, with Gorgias "worth the premium when 40%+ of your tickets need real Shopify actions" — [Lindy: Gorgias Pricing 2026](https://www.lindy.ai/blog/gorgias-pricing)

### Inferences
- Gorgias's unlimited-seats-but-pay-per-ticket model is structurally different from every other platform researched (all of which charge per seat); for a small, volunteer-heavy operation like Freegle with many part-time human moderators but low ticket volume, a per-ticket model could in principle be cheaper — but Gorgias is built specifically for Shopify/e-commerce order-management actions, which don't map to Freegle's freecycling use case.
- The peak-season overage spike (2.7–3.5×) is a general warning for any usage-metered AI support pricing, not unique to Gorgias — Freegle should budget for variance, not just an average.

### Gaps
- No nonprofit discount information found for Gorgias.
- No detail found on Gorgias's clarifying-question behaviour or structured-attribute extraction beyond "resolves per interaction."

## Plain (plain.com)

### Takeaway
Plain is an API-first B2B support platform for technical teams, splitting AI into a customer-facing agent ("Ari," included free on all plans, no per-resolution fee — a notable contrast to Fin/Zendesk/Gorgias) and an agent-assist copilot ("Sidekick," metered by monthly credits); published pricing is inconsistent across Plain's own materials ($35 vs $39 vs $199 for the entry tier) and a Startup Program offers up to 50% off.

### Cited Findings
- Official page lists three plans: "Foundation at $35/month, Horizon at $299/month, and custom Frontier" — billed per seat, monthly or annual (~10% off annual); 7-day free trial, no credit card — [Plain: Pricing](https://www.plain.com/pricing)
- "Ari is an AI agent that talks to customers and resolves issues for you, across any channel," and is "included on all Plain plans with no additional fees per resolution, unlike many competitors who charge per ticket resolved by AI" — [Plain comparison blog, via search aggregation](https://www.plain.com/blog/api-first-support-platforms)
- "Sidekick is a copilot for human agents that suggests responses and accesses customer context" and *is* credit-metered: "If you use up your monthly credit allowance, Sidekick is paused until your credits reset... or until you purchase an add-on credit pack. The rest of Plain keeps working normally." — [Plain product/pricing pages, via search aggregation]
- Plain says it has an "API-first model, guaranteeing infinite extensibility... its public API existed before the user interface, so every feature can be used programmatically" — aimed at technical, product-led companies; one review warns it "might feel rigid or pricey for smaller or non-technical teams" — [Software Finder: Plain pricing/features](https://softwarefinder.com/customer-service-software/plain)
- A Startup Program offers "eligible early-stage startups up to 50% off" — [Plain: Pricing](https://www.plain.com/pricing)
- Sources disagree on entry price and trial length across different Plain-published pages/dates: Software Finder cites Foundation $39/mo (1 seat), 14-day trial; Plain's Aug 2025 blog also says $39/agent monthly, 14-day trial; Plain's June 2026 API blog calls the entry tier "Foundation ($199/mo flat)"; the most recent comparison post (dated as current to July 2026) matches the official pricing page's $35/month, 7-day trial — [aggregated across plain.com blog posts, dates as cited in search]

## Recurring design patterns: ticket hierarchy, AI-filled attributes, slot filling, confidence thresholds, human-in-the-loop

### Takeaway
Across vendors, a "conversation → ticket → problem/incident" hierarchy is a manual/semi-manual linking exercise even at market leaders (Zendesk); AI-driven attribute extraction is implemented as natural-language "read this attribute, or ask for it" instructions (Fin) or discrete "collect data" steps (Fin Tasks/Workflows) rather than classic slot-filling forms; confidence thresholds are widely used to gate autonomous reply vs. escalation but are explicitly criticised by some vendors as an unreliable single signal, with sentiment, explicit human requests, repeated failure, and sensitive-topic rules layered on top as parallel escalation triggers.

### Cited Findings
- **Problem/Incident hierarchy is manual, not AI-linked:** In Zendesk, an agent manually marks a ticket "Problem" and manually sets/links other tickets as "Incident"; solving the Problem auto-solves linked Incidents and propagates the resolving comment; explicitly, "AI agent tickets can't be linked to problem and incident tickets," and no AI auto-linking feature was found — [Zendesk Help: Working with problem and incident tickets](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408835103898-Working-with-problem-and-incident-tickets)
- **Dedup is same-requester-only or history-matched, not general clustering:** Zendesk Copilot's merge suggestions match "by requester (not content similarity)"; Linear's Triage Intelligence "flags likely related issues or duplicates based on your workspace's history" (a content/embedding-style match against past issues, unlike Zendesk); Gorgias has a *billing* dedup rule (a resolution that escalates to a human within 72 hours is billed only once, as a ticket, not as a resolution too) — [eesel AI: Zendesk merge tickets](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/zendesk-merge-tickets-duplicate-conversations); [Linear: AI](https://linear.app/ai); [Lindy: Gorgias Pricing 2026](https://www.lindy.ai/blog/gorgias-pricing)
- **AI-filled attributes as instructions, not forms:** Intercom Fin's Procedures collect data via plain-language instructions ("@read email. If it is not known, ask the user for their email before continuing") plus validation instructions, and Data Connector responses auto-populate "temporary attributes" referenceable later in the conversation; the older, more form-like "Collect data" step still exists in legacy Workflows/Fin Tasks — [Fin Help: How to write instructions in Fin Procedures](https://fin.ai/help/en/articles/13976256-how-to-write-instructions-in-fin-procedures); [Fin Help: attributes/data connectors](https://fin.ai/help/en/articles/13975853-how-attributes-can-power-your-fin-tasks-escalations-workflows-and-data-connectors)
- **Confidence-scored classification for triage gating:** Zendesk's Intelligent Triage assigns High/Medium/Low confidence to each intent/sentiment/language prediction, explicitly so teams can "automate on high-confidence tickets and send the rest to human review" — [Zendesk Help: intent/sentiment/language](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4550640560538-Automatically-classifying-customer-intent-sentiment-and-language)
- **Confidence thresholds for reply-vs-escalate, with dual thresholds:** "most platforms let administrators set confidence thresholds, so the AI answers autonomously above a high threshold and escalates below a lower one" — some vendors (Decagon, per this source) route low-confidence responses to *asynchronous human pre-review* rather than immediate escalation, trading latency for quality on non-live channels — [Twig: How AI Escalates to Humans](https://www.twig.so/blog/how-does-ai-know-when-to-escalate-to-human-agent); [Decagon glossary: AI escalation policy, via aggregation](https://decagon.ai/glossary/what-is-an-ai-escalation-policy)
- **Explicit skepticism of confidence scores as the primary signal:** My AskAI argues "the number isn't reliable... For a generative AI agent that number is half guesswork... What decides a handoff is whether the AI can ground an answer, and how easy you make the way to a human," proposing instead: "answer; if it can't, clarify; and if it still can't, offer a human." eesel similarly argues the real design failure is a customer "feeling stuck" / "trapped," not a mistuned numeric threshold — [My AskAI: AI Confidence Thresholds](https://myaskai.com/blog/ai-confidence-thresholds-handoff); [eesel AI: AI agent handoff best practices 2026](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/ai-agent-handoff-best-practices)
- **Parallel/overriding escalation triggers cited across sources:** explicit customer request for a human ("immediate and unconditional... no scenario where an AI agent should continue... after a clear, explicit request"); repeated failure ("after two or three failed resolution attempts... continued AI handling is unlikely to produce a different result"); negative sentiment dropping below a threshold; and hard business-rule carve-outs regardless of confidence — "billing and refund disputes, account security concerns, legal or compliance matters, cancellation and churn risk" always route to a human — [Capacity: AI Agent Escalation](https://capacity.com/blog/ai-agent-escalation/); synthesis across [eesel AI](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/ai-agent-handoff-best-practices) and [Twig](https://www.twig.so/blog/how-does-ai-know-when-to-escalate-to-human-agent)
- **Context handoff quality is the most commonly cited failure point:** "the most common reason a handoff feels broken: the AI passes the ticket but not the story. The human agent should get the full conversation history, what the AI already attempted, the customer's account and order details, the reason it escalated, and ideally a suggested next step." — [eesel AI: AI agent handoff best practices 2026](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/ai-agent-handoff-best-practices)
- **Unified customer identity as a precondition for the whole pipeline:** Decagon describes it as "a single source of truth that ties phone numbers, email addresses, account IDs, and device IDs to one customer record"; identity resolution in practice combines "deterministic and probabilistic matching" to stitch channel identifiers to one persistent ID, with real-time profile enrichment as new events occur; a bad merge is explicitly flagged as a data-protection/correctness risk ("may expose another customer's history or trigger the wrong service decision") — [Decagon glossary: omnichannel customer support, via aggregation](https://decagon.ai/glossary/what-is-omni-channel-customer-support); [Infobip: Omnichannel Customer Data](https://www.infobip.com/blog/omnichannel-customer-data); [Udesk: Omnichannel strategy](https://www.udeskglobal.com/blog/omnichannel-customer-service-strategy-how-to-unify-context-routing-and-reporting.html)
- **Three hub architectures for unifying identity:** "Helpdesk as hub" (Intercom/Zendesk/Freshdesk/HubSpot/Salesforce Service Cloud — best when the team is centred on support), "CDP as hub" (Segment/RudderStack/mParticle — best for sophisticated cross-functional data needs), "CRM as hub" (Salesforce/HubSpot — best when the relationship spans sales/service/marketing) — [open.cx: Omnichannel Customer Service Guide 2026](https://www.open.cx/blog/omnichannel-customer-service)

### Inferences
- The clearest, most repeated pattern across every vendor researched is: AI is trusted to *gather* information and *classify/score confidence* fairly readily, but is trusted to *link disparate reports into one incident/problem* far less — that linking step stays manual (Zendesk) or is limited to matching against a single tenant's own historical issue corpus (Linear) rather than true cross-report clustering. Freegle should expect to build or curate this "linking" step itself rather than finding it as a mature off-the-shelf AI feature anywhere.
- The industry is visibly split on whether a single numeric confidence threshold is good design at all — vendors selling the escalation/handoff layer itself (My AskAI, eesel) are the ones warning against over-relying on it, which is a useful counter-argument to keep in mind if Freegle's own design leans on a single score. A safer design synthesis from the sources: combine a confidence/sentiment score with hard rule-based overrides (explicit human request, repeated-failure count, sensitive-topic list) and always pass structured context (not just "the ticket") on handoff.
- Freegle, as a single small charity, doesn't need a general "CDP" or "CRM as hub" identity architecture — the "helpdesk as hub" pattern is the natural fit, but since Freegle's forum (Discourse) isn't natively unified by any helpdesk vendor (see prior section), it would need its own lightweight identity-stitching (e.g. matching Discourse user account to email/ticket) rather than inheriting one from a vendor.

### Gaps
- No vendor was found publishing the *actual numeric* confidence thresholds it uses by default (e.g., "escalate below 0.6") — all sources describe the mechanism in the abstract, not concrete production values.
- No first-party technical documentation on how any vendor's dedup/duplicate-detection actually clusters semantically similar-but-differently-worded reports (embedding similarity, keyword matching, etc.) was found — this would need deeper docs research (e.g., Linear's or eesel's own technical blog posts) beyond this pass.

## Published outcomes/resolution rates and known failure modes

### Takeaway
Vendor-published resolution rates (Intercom Fin ~71–76%, Gorgias 26–56% reviewer-estimated) are inflated relative to independently observed real-world rates (Fin ~38–53% in small-scale/third-party tests) partly because of definitional choices (e.g., counting "the customer went quiet" as a resolution, or excluding rule-constrained conversations from the denominator); peer-reviewed research confirms hallucinated or wrong AI answers measurably damage customer trust, word-of-mouth, and loyalty, and 53–77% of surveyed customers report a frustrating chatbot experience.

### Cited Findings
- Intercom/Fin's own claimed average resolution rate is 76% (March 2026 blog, and Fin's own site), but Fin's *own* other pages disagree: a guarantee page says "grown from 30% to 76% since launch" and a Sierra-comparison page says "71% across 7,000+ customers" — both live in August 2026 — [Intercom Blog: From resolutions to outcomes](https://www.intercom.com/blog/from-resolutions-to-outcomes-evolving-how-fin-delivers-value/)
- Intercom changed its resolution-rate *methodology* in 2026 to exclude "Fin Constrained" conversations (where escalation rules limited Fin) from the denominator, which mechanically increases the reported rate; restated data only extends back to 1 October 2025 — [Intercom Help: Update to Fin performance metrics](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/15599377-update-to-fin-performance-metrics)
- Independent/third-party tests found materially lower rates: Builts.ai's own 60-day test across 4 small-business clients (500 tickets/month combined) averaged 38% versus a "marketed 50%"; a competitor (CloneDesk) claims real-world production rates cluster at 45–53%, with complex B2B queues at the low end — [Builts.ai / CloneDesk, via getmacha.com aggregation](https://www.getmacha.com/blog/intercom-fin-ai-explained)
- Gorgias reviewer-estimated automation rate: 26–56% of tickets resolved by AI, with Gorgias framed as "worth the premium when 40%+ of your tickets need real Shopify actions" — [Lindy: Gorgias Pricing 2026](https://www.lindy.ai/blog/gorgias-pricing)
- Peer-reviewed research on hallucination harm: "When AI Fails" (Belanche, Casaló & Flavián, SAGE Journals, June 2026) found that "hallucinations substantially increase negative WOM and erode customer loyalty," that customers "feel confused and frustrated and begin to question the professionalism of the firm," and cites a survey where "73% of practitioners cite inaccurate responses as their biggest concern (Thomson Reuters, 2025)" and the real-world Air Canada case where the airline "had to pay more than US$800" after its chatbot quoted an incorrect fare — [SAGE Journals: When AI Fails](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00472875261456334)
- A second peer-reviewed study ("LLM Hallucinations in Conversational AI for Customer Service," Taylor & Francis) found hallucination types cause frustration via "an inefficient customer experience where users would have to rephrase questions and try again, double-check the information or contact a human," quoting a participant: "ignoring customer requests erodes trust and confidence" — [Taylor & Francis: LLM Hallucinations in Conversational AI](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10447318.2025.2580540)
- UC Berkeley's California Management Review (April 2026) reports "multiple surveys show that 53-77% of survey respondents have had a bad or frustrating experience of interacting with chatbots," warning that "hefty cost savings for businesses may lead to significant hidden costs for customers and consequently businesses as well" — [CMR Berkeley: Chatbot Frustration is Real](https://cmr.berkeley.edu/2026/04/chatbot-frustration-is-real-hidden-costs-and-best-practices/)
- Vendor-reported (lower-confidence, self-interested) hallucination-rate figures: IrisAgent claims "ungrounded large language models hallucinate in 15-30% of customer service responses, depending on query complexity" and an "8-12% CSAT lift on tickets where the AI cited its sources"; Tendem.ai cites second-hand stats of "15–27%" hallucination rates in live interactions (attributed to "SQ Magazine 2026") and a claim that "39% of AI-powered customer service bots were pulled back or significantly reworked due to hallucination-related errors" (attributed to "Testlio 2025") — flagged by the search itself as lower-reliability, vendor-sourced, and in one case apparently illustrative rather than a real documented incident — [Tendem.ai: True Cost of AI Hallucinations](https://tendem.ai/blog/true-cost-ai-hallucinations-business-data)
- Common industry-recommended mitigations converge on the same three practices: "ground every chatbot response in your verified knowledge base, validate answers against source documents before sending, and route low-confidence queries to human agents," plus ensuring easy human handoff — "A chatbot without human handoff is a trap. Customers get stuck, loop, and leave frustrated." — [Social Intents: AI Chatbot Hallucination in Customer Service 2026](https://www.socialintents.com/blog/ai-chatbot-hallucination-in-customer-service/)
- A legal precedent from early 2026: a user sued after a generative AI app gave an incorrect answer and "promised" compensation if wrong; the Hangzhou Internet Court dismissed the claim, "emphasizing warnings and safeguards in their fault-based liability framework" — [via search aggregation, court case summary]

### Inferences
- Any resolution-rate figure Freegle sees quoted by a vendor should be treated as a best-case ceiling reached under favourable conditions (comprehensive knowledge base, generous definition of "resolved," rule-constrained conversations excluded), not a number a small charity with a thinner knowledge base and messier community-report format should expect to replicate — the Builts.ai 38%-vs-50%-marketed gap is the most concrete evidence of this.
- The peer-reviewed literature is unambiguous that hallucinated or wrong bot answers actively harm trust and word-of-mouth (a real risk for a volunteer charity that relies heavily on community goodwill and reputation), which strengthens the case for keeping AI clarification/triage strictly upstream of human decision-making rather than allowing autonomous customer-facing resolution on Freegle's use cases (e.g. never auto-closing a moderation or safety report without human sign-off).

### Gaps
- No published resolution-rate figures were found for Help Scout AI Answers, Front Autopilot, Freshdesk Freddy AI Agent, Plain's Ari, Pylon, or Atlassian JSM's virtual agent — only Intercom Fin and (via reviewer estimate) Gorgias had rate figures surface in this research pass.
- No rigorous, vendor-independent, large-sample hallucination-rate study (as opposed to vendor blog claims or the narrower academic studies on customer *perception* of hallucination) was found.

## Nonprofit pricing programmes (rollup)

### Takeaway
Nonprofit pricing across the researched vendors ranges from a genuinely simple, published, stackable discount (Help Scout: 10%) to purely discretionary/undocumented sales conversations (Intercom Fin: no published percentage, historically US-only) to competitive grant programmes that most applicants won't win (Zendesk Tech for Good: ~110 partner orgs globally, no general discount) to no evidence of any programme at all (Front, Gorgias, Plain has only a general Startup Program, Pylon/Linear/Decagon/Sierra — enterprise vendors with no nonprofit angle found).

### Cited Findings
- **Help Scout:** published, standard 10% nonprofit discount, explicitly "combined with the annual payment discount"; a separate, less-defined "Help Scout for Good" program exists for "qualifying non-profits and B Corporations" with unverified (potentially much larger) discounts claimed by third parties — [Help Scout Support: Billing and Plans Guide](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/596-billing-and-plans-guide)
- **Intercom:** no published percentage; "US-based nonprofit organizations may be eligible," verification process historically only workable for US organizations per a 2023 staff community reply, contact-sales-directly process — [Intercom Help: Early Stage program](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/9361928-early-stage-program)
- **Zendesk:** no standard nonprofit discount; instead a competitive "Tech for Good" grant/software programme (110+ partner nonprofits, Impact Awards of $5,000–$50,000), gated to specific cause areas, excludes current/past customers and grantees, 2026 application window already closed (due 15 July 2026) — [Zendesk Tech for Good: Impact Awards](https://techforgood.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/6476814314637-What-are-the-Tech-for-Good-Impact-Awards)
- **Freshdesk/Freshworks:** conflicting reports from 10–20% to 90% off; one real user report describes only "a fairly minimal discount" after a sales call; discounts can't be combined and it's unconfirmed whether they cover AI add-ons — [Freshworks Community: NPO/Non Profit Pricing](https://community.freshworks.com/getting-started-11331/npo-non-profit-pricing-39008)
- **Plain:** no nonprofit-specific program found; only a general Startup Program (up to 50% off for eligible early-stage, for-profit-oriented startups, not nonprofit-specific) — [Plain: Pricing](https://www.plain.com/pricing)
- **Front, Gorgias, Pylon, Linear, Atlassian JSM, Decagon, Sierra:** no nonprofit discount programme surfaced in this research pass for any of these seven vendors.

### Inferences
- Of all vendors researched, Help Scout is the only one offering an unambiguous, published, no-application, stackable nonprofit discount — making it the easiest nonprofit pricing story to plan around, even though it's a modest 10%.
- For every other vendor with any nonprofit angle (Intercom, Zendesk, Freshdesk), the real process is "apply/ask and see what you get," with outcomes ranging from meaningful (Zendesk's rare, competitive grants) to negligible (one Freshworks user's real experience) — Freegle should not budget around an assumed nonprofit discount for any of these without first getting a specific written quote.

### Gaps
- No UK-specific nonprofit/charity pricing terms were found for any vendor (all discussion of "nonprofit" discounts in the sources is US-centric, e.g. 501(c)(3) documentation); it is unconfirmed whether a UK-registered charity like Freegle would even qualify for Intercom's or Freshworks' nonprofit processes.
- No nonprofit programme information exists to find for Front, Gorgias, Pylon, Linear, Atlassian, Decagon, or Sierra — treated as a confirmed absence for the enterprise-only vendors (Decagon, Sierra), but a genuine research gap (not confirmed absent, just not found) for Front, Gorgias, Pylon, Linear, and Atlassian JSM.
